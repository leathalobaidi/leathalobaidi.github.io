From the CMA to Consultancy

Part 6 of 7: Five former CMA leaders and economists, six career moves, and the different jobs firms recruited them to do.

Leath · Updated 14 November 2026

Stefan Hunt became a member of AlixPartners UK LLP on 2 December 2025. The firm’s announcement the next day explained the job: he would lead its new Behavioural Economics and Technology Evidence team. His previous employer was Keystone Strategy. Before that, he had built data and technology capabilities at the Competition and Markets Authority.

Keystone had announced Hunt as head of its European Advanced Technology Services team, with an intended start of 24 January 2023. Two consultancies had hired the same former regulator for related expertise in less than three years.

The receiving firms

The five people below took different routes out of the CMA. Their receiving firms’ announcements describe management, merger analysis, data and advisory roles. Hunt appears twice because his later move was between two private firms. This is a selected set of career events, not a count of all departures from the authority.

For the firms, the appeal includes practical knowledge of how economic evidence is tested. Oxera’s announcement points to Sala’s work on merger investigations. AlixPartners describes Bon’s responsibility for the quality of economic analysis across CMA cases. Such experience can complement the technical skills a consultancy already has; the announcements do not disclose what it paid to acquire it.

Building on an existing practice

Keystone’s European expansion brought Coscelli and Hunt into a team co-led by Cristina Caffarra. Its recruitment of Hunt joined economics to data science and technology. By December 2025, AlixPartners was recruiting him for a new specialist team within its own economics practice.

AlixPartners had already announced the arrival of Nicola Mazzarotto and Caitlin Wilkinson from KPMG on 3 October 2024. The release described an established European economics practice and said it had doubled in size over the preceding three years. These hires were an expansion. The firm was not entering economics for the first time.

The October 2024 release establishes the announcement date for both recruits. It leaves their respective first working days unconfirmed.

This sequence shows how a firm can add complementary experience through several appointments. It does not establish that an investor directed the hiring or that a smaller partnership could not have afforded it. The ownership transactions in Part 5 address a different question: who supplies capital and who receives the proceeds.

Two dates for Joe Perkins

A membership register can put a career move in the wrong year if its dates are read too casually. Compass Lexecon announced Joe Perkins’s appointment on 14 April 2020; FTI’s release followed on 22 April. Ofgem’s chief economist would become a Senior Vice President and Head of Research in London from June.

Companies House records his FTI Consulting LLP membership from 4 June 2024. That is a later legal appointment in the same corporate family. Counting it as a new departure from Ofgem would shift the employment move by four years.

The distinction matters beyond Perkins. A professional title alone does not establish the date of a statutory appointment. Appointment announcements and biographies help fill that gap, although they too may give a publication date rather than a first working day. Part 7 keeps those event types and sources beside each named move.

What the departures leave unanswered

The selected announcements show firms recruiting people with substantial regulatory experience. They say much less about the CMA’s capacity after those people left. Assessing that would require its incoming recruits, internal promotions, staffing and workload over the same period. A list of prominent exits cannot establish a net drain.

The useful question is how quickly they can replace the particular judgement and experience that clients seek. Hunt’s successive appointments show demand for those skills; the cost of replenishing them remains harder to observe.
