The Business of Economic Advice

Who earns the money when companies buy economic advice? Eleven pieces follow the firms, their owners and their people from 2000 to 2026.

One member of RBB Economics was entitled to £5.77m of profit in the year to March 2025. The group accounts leave the member unnamed. They show a £60.2m pool available for division. Divide that pool by the average membership of 18 and the result is £3.35m per average member, before personal tax.

Economics consultancies sell advice on mergers, regulation, damages and policy. This series asks how ownership and the movement of senior economists shape the rewards. Start with the profits, follow the history, or inspect the data.

The research catalogue contains 90 firms and practices. In the 24 July 2026 financial snapshot, checked against source accounts in September, 30 selected entities report a combined £2.44bn in revenue across year-ends from July 2024 to December 2025. The selection mixes activities, geographies and accounting bases. The methodology explains the boundaries and sources.

The numbered history (seven parts)
Part 1 · 2000

Who Was Here in 2000

Macroeconomic forecasting and advice on regulation gave Britain’s economics consultancies two overlapping origins. The opening chapter traces the institutions, firms and people who connected them.

Part 2 · 2000–2010

The Founders’ Decade, 2000–2010

RBB and Frontier chose different ways to share ownership. Their founding stories show how an economist’s decision to leave a firm can become a decision about how to run one.

Part 3 · 2010–2015

Collapse, Capital and New Entrants, 2010–2015

LECG’s collapse released teams into rival firms. Capital Economics attracted outside investment. Specialist entrants added further ways to organise and sell economic advice.

Part 4 · 2015–2020

London Goes Global, 2015–2020

International advisers expanded in London as owners bought and sold consulting businesses. Brexit added a plausible source of demand, though the accounts cannot isolate its effect.

Part 5 · 2020–2026

The Profit Machine

RBB and Baringa illustrate the rewards of partnership. Vivid, PA Consulting and Econic show different routes to outside capital, and why a deal valuation tells us less about individual wealth than it first appears.

Part 6 · CMA → private sector

From the CMA to Consultancy

Stefan Hunt’s route ran through Keystone before AlixPartners. Other senior CMA economists also took private-sector roles. Their dated moves reveal demand for experience, without measuring a net drain from the regulator.

Part 7 · Network

The People Network

A sourced selection of career moves connects the institutions in the history. Each event has a name, date and destination, so readers can follow the paths behind the argument.
