UK Economics Consultancy: A Historical Series

Eleven pieces on UK economics consultancy from 2000 to 2026, built around a curated 90-firm project catalogue, filed accounts where available, and dated public sources.

The project catalogue tracks 90 firms associated with UK economics consultancy. It is a curated research directory, not a census of every firm operating in the sector. The industry serves corporate clients, law firms, regulators and government bodies, a client base also described in Cebr’s 2018 sector study. The series examines competition, regulation, litigation and public-policy work through selected firms, people and events; it does not treat those examples as a complete measure of the market.

In the interactive explorer, 30 selected legal entities with positive turnover observations total £2.4418bn (£2.44bn). These are the latest available figures for each selected entity, with year-ends from July 2024 to December 2025, not one common filed year. Two foreign-currency rows use analytical conversion assumptions of €1=£0.85 and $1=£0.78. The result is gross reported turnover of the selected entities, including non-economics activity inside broad and litigation consultancies; it is neither UK sector revenue nor a lower bound for it.

For comparison, Cebr reported £1.53bn of sector revenue for 2016/17 and an 11.3% nominal three-year average growth rate. Mechanically extending that historical rate for eight years gives £3.60bn for 2024/25. That calculation is the author’s scenario, not a Cebr forecast or an upper bound.

The history runs from 2000 to 2026. It uses filed Companies House accounts where separate accounts are available, together with cited announcements, firm histories, obituaries, press coverage and selected Global Competition Review material. The supporting dataset, code and source archive are not yet available as a complete public release. Interpretive descriptions below are identified as the article’s thesis, hypothesis or commentary. The numbered history (seven parts)
Part 1 · 2000

Who Was Here in 2000

The article’s thesis is that the market had not yet taken its modern shape. It develops that interpretation through a selected portrait of four firms, 14 people and three influential cases; those counts are not market totals.

Part 2 · 2000–2010

The Founders' Decade

NERA was one of the established firms. Derek Ridyard, Simon Bishop and Simon Baker went on to establish RBB Economics in 2002, as RBB’s history records. In the project’s small comparable LLP sample, RBB’s FY2025 profit available to members of £60.226m divided by 18 average members gives £3.346m per member, the highest disclosed row.

Part 3 · 2010–2015

The Professionalisation, PE Arrives

LECG collapsed in 2011; Compass Lexecon announced that it would acquire LECG’s European competition team of more than 40. In October 2014, LDC invested in Capital Economics. The article interprets that transaction as an early sign of greater investor interest.

Part 4 · 2015–2020

The Global Arms Race

The article documents London expansion by US-linked advisory groups and a second major private-equity transaction in 2018, after LDC’s 2014 investment in Capital Economics. It treats a possible link between Brexit and regulatory-advisory growth as a hypothesis, not a demonstrated sector-wide causal effect.

Part 5 · 2020–2026

The Profit Machine

RBB’s FY2025 accounts report 18 average members and a registered office at 199 Bishopsgate; they do not establish exclusive occupancy of a whole floor. McKinsey’s acquisition of Vivid Economics and Planetrics, Jacobs’ acquisition of the remaining PA Consulting shares in March 2026, and Econic’s disclosed financing show three distinct ownership or funding routes. The article labels “acqui-hire” and “breakaway” as interpretations.

Part 6 · CMA → private sector

The CMA Drain

Five named senior Competition and Markets Authority economists moved to private-sector roles between January 2023 and January 2026. The documented sequence for Stefan Hunt is CMA, Keystone Strategy and AlixPartners. Examples include Frontier Economics’ announcement of Mike Walker and AlixPartners’ announcement of Julie Bon.

Part 7 · Network

The People Network

A chord diagram of 19 aggregated firm-to-firm ribbons covering selected senior moves, 2000–2026. Ribbon weights can combine more than one person and seniority, so the chart does not claim a reproducible count of individual moves. It is the visible senior-move layer, not every career move in the market.
