AI for an Economist

How I used Claude Code and OpenAI Codex to build the catalogue, check the accounts and turn the research into a series.

Leath Al Obaidi · Research and evidence audit · Revised November 2026

RBB Economics’ accounts for the year to March 2025 report £60.2m of profit available to members. Divide it by the average membership of 18 and the result is £3.35m. How did a small group of economics advisers build such a profitable business?

I used Claude Code and local scripts to assemble the initial catalogue, charts and drafts. OpenAI Codex helped review the evidence, reconcile the published records and prepare the revised series and database. The catalogue covers 90 firms and practices. The saved records provide no measured comparison with doing the work by hand.

AI helped write scripts, search announcements and draft prose. It also joined facts that should have stayed apart. The useful lesson is in the distance between collecting an observation and knowing what it supports.

What is being counted?

The catalogue is a research directory of businesses associated with UK economics consultancy. It includes specialist advisers, forecasting houses, policy firms and practices within larger groups. Its 90 entries are neither a census nor 90 independent companies with comparable accounts. Some brands share a filing entity; some practices file only within a parent.

Categories help readers navigate that mixed population. They are editorial choices, not an official industry classification or a rule that economics must be every firm’s principal product. Broad consultancies remain useful comparators, provided their full revenue is not presented as revenue from economic advice.

The maps answer different questions. The UK map has 80 dated registered-address rows from July and September 2026, including related entities, historical entries and one contextual firm outside the catalogue. The international views use a separate selection of ten firms, with 109 city observations checked against their office and contact pages on 12 September 2026. Neither is a headcount map. The selected career events in Part 7 are another unit again: a named move supported by a dated source.

From accounts to comparisons

The financial selection was frozen on 24 July 2026. It takes each entity’s latest row in that research extract and retains a revenue figure after a September check against the original accounts. Thirty rows qualify. CRA UK, Analysis Group, Capital Economics and AlixPartners filed newer accounts after this cutoff. The 30 reporting periods end between July 2024 and December 2025, and the selected revenue observations sum to £2,441,511,439.47. The precision allows the calculation to be reproduced; £2.44bn is ample for the prose.

That sum mixes geographical coverage and activities. It also includes AlixPartners’ net revenue after reimbursable expenses, alongside other firms’ reported revenue. The two foreign-currency observations use fixed analytical conversions of €1=£0.85 and $1=£0.78. They are not exchange rates taken from the filings. The total is unsuitable as either the size of the UK economics-consultancy market or a lower bound for it.

Cebr’s March 2018 study estimated sector revenue of £1.53bn for 2016/17 and nominal average growth of 11.3% over three years. Extending that historical rate for eight years gives £3.60bn for 2024/25. This is a mechanical scenario calculated for this series, not a new observation, a Cebr forecast or an upper bound. The two totals cannot be combined into a market-size range.

Profit requires its own definitions. Operating profit measures the result at one stage of the accounts. Profit available to LLP members may be reported after remuneration charged as an expense. Some firms charge the full member allocation before reporting a zero residual. A zero in that position does not mean their partners worked for nothing.

The comparison of profit per member therefore uses four source-checked positive annual profit observations with average membership: RBB, Baringa, Oxera and Macro Advisory Partners. Fideres is excluded because its recorded amount owed to members is a balance-sheet liability, not annual profit. Each figure is the residual annual profit pool divided by average membership. Macro Advisory Partners’ pool is £312,000 after members’ remuneration, rather than the £4,279,000 before that charge. It is a small comparison of accounting observations, not a ranking of every consultancy’s partner pay. Blank fields mean missing or non-comparable data; they are not silently turned into zero.

Reading one filing properly

RBB’s FY2025 filed accounts show revenue of £108,402,487, operating profit of £60,988,932 and profit available for members’ discretionary division of £60,226,315. The income statement is on printed page 9; note 8 on printed page 21 gives 18 average members. The operating margin is 56.3%. The final pool divided by 18 is £3,345,906, rounded here to £3.35m.

The arithmetic is the easy part. The consolidation policy includes overseas subsidiaries, and the revenue note splits income by destination. A London registered address cannot turn that group result into the output of one London office. Nor can the average allocation tell us each member’s income, personal tax or hours worked.

The explorer exposes the legal entity, reporting period, accounting basis and source beside the financial observations. Estimates are identified: Baringa’s top-member figure, for example, applies a disclosed 7% share to the profit pool and excludes the separate share of disposal cash described in the note.

What the software did

Both tools worked with local files and scripts. The Companies House API supplied company records and filing histories. Downloaded accounts held the financial detail. SQLite and CSV tables kept names, dates and numbers in a form that could feed the charts.

Research agents searched groups of firms in parallel. Their output was a queue of possible claims and sources. A company announcement could establish that a hire had been announced; it did not necessarily establish the person’s first working day. An LLP appointment could date legal membership years after someone joined the business.

A claim about a named person needs that distinction before it enters the prose or a diagram. The current people view records selected events without weights. It does not convert a launch roster into a count of departures, or a handful of senior hires into a measure of the regulator’s net staff loss.

Checking the result

File checks can confirm that a source exists, a link works or a calculation reproduces. They cannot, on their own, show that it supports the sentence. The initial 11 September revision used a targeted 30-claim audit. The 12 September review expanded to the whole series: prose, captions, timelines, metadata and the values behind the charts. The register preserves the original propositions, the source passages read and the reasons for correcting, qualifying or removing them. These are editorial judgements, not a measured error rate for the industry or for AI.

What remains outside the evidence

The evidence used here is desk research: filed accounts, published histories and dated announcements. No interview material is used to establish the claims, and the series offers no claim to know private negotiations or motives. Transactions can reveal announced valuations and payment terms. They seldom disclose enough to reconstruct every owner’s proceeds.

The selected accounts cannot establish sector-wide growth, and the selected career moves cannot establish retention or net workforce flows. Those questions would require a defined population and observations over time. The public database provides selected firm, people, financial and location records with source links, plus CSV, JSON and SQLite downloads. The full research archive, working notes and copies of the original sources remain private.

For another economist attempting similar work, I would begin with one filing and one question. Preserve the page, identify the accounting line and reproduce the calculation before expanding the list. A larger database is useful only if its smallest observation can bear scrutiny.
