VIEW 1 · REGISTERED ADDRESSES

Registered offices in Britain

The map plots 80 address rows: 53 in London and 27 elsewhere. A registered office is an address for legal correspondence; it need not be where the staff work. These rows include related entities and historical entries. Each popup gives the source date. Pins are approximate illustrations, not verified address coordinates.

UK registered office addresses
Source: Companies House · 80 dated registered-address observations · July and September 2026

The City and its fringe contain many familiar names. FTI Consulting appears on Aldersgate Street in the July records; three Alvarez & Marsal entities share 30 Old Bailey. September checks place RBB at 199 Bishopsgate and Frontier at Worship Square. Baringa and Capital Economics appear farther west, near Westminster. The pattern is visible. The reasons for choosing each address are not in the filings.

Oxford supplies four of the non-London rows: Oxera, Oxford Economics, AFRY Management Consulting and SQW. Cambridge has Cambridge Econometrics and RAND Europe, the latter included for context outside the main catalogue. Other points reach Edinburgh, Glasgow, Newcastle, Liverpool and smaller towns.

Coverage of the UK map

The 80 rows do not equal 80 distinct active catalogue firms. They include three A&M entities, two Capital Economics companies and two ECA names, as well as Vivid Economics and Indepen Consulting, retained as historical records. NEF Consulting and Skylark have filed London addresses but are absent from this map.

Nine catalogue entries lack a separately recorded UK address in this export: BDO, Deloitte, Grant Thornton, PwC, RSM, E.CA Economics, Fraser of Allander, PA Consulting and Keystone Strategy. This is a limit of the directory, not proof that a firm has no UK operation. The international directory below uses a different selection of ten firms.

Where footprints overlap

The international directory compares ten firms using their published office and contact lists, checked on 12 September 2026. A line joins two when their recorded footprints share at least the number of cities chosen on the slider. The default is three.

Firms with extensive networks have more opportunities to overlap. A single-city firm cannot acquire a line at the default threshold, however busy its business. Moving the slider changes the rule, not the market. The graph contains locations, not client relationships or staff transfers.
