The Profit Machine

Part 5 of 7: RBB’s accounts reveal what a profitable specialist can earn. Baringa, PA and Econic show how differently the proceeds can be divided.

Leath · Updated 14 November 2026

One member of RBB Economics was entitled to £5,771,775 of profit in the year to March 2025. The figure appears in Note 8 of accounts filed on 23 January 2026. The recipient is unnamed. The entitlement alone is enough to explain why the ownership of an economics consultancy matters.

RBB’s consolidated group earned £108.4m in revenue and £61.0m in operating profit. After interest and corporate tax, £60.2m was available for division among an average of 18 members: about £3.35m per member. This is a group profit ratio, before members’ personal tax, rather than an equal pay award. The business includes overseas operations and averaged 269 employees as well as its members.

RBB has the highest such ratio among the four latest observations with disclosed discretionary profit and average membership in this project’s detailed-account LLP sample. Its economics become more interesting beside a much larger partnership.

Who shares the growth?

In FY2025 RBB’s revenue rose by £26.4m and operating profit by £17.0m. Average membership stayed at 18. That combination lifted the profit available per member sharply, even though these figures do not separate the contributions of prices, utilisation and case mix.

Baringa’s group accounts for the year to 31 March 2025 describe a broader consulting business with £449.8m of revenue, 178 average members and £161.9m of discretionary profit. Dividing that pool by average membership gives about £910,000 per average member. Baringa earns much more in total than RBB, but shares its profit across nearly ten times as many members.

The average also conceals a wide distribution. Baringa’s Note 12 assigns its highest-entitlement member 7% of the financial-year profit, implying an estimated £11.3m from the £161.9m pool. It separately assigns that member 8% of cash received in connection with a business disposal. The £11.3m is therefore neither a disclosed absolute nor the member’s full total including that payment.

These are different organisations, serving different mixes of work. Their accounts do establish a simple constraint on partner economics: growth in total profit need not produce comparable growth in the amount available per member. The number sharing the pool matters too.

What the margin leaves out

An LLP’s operating profit can sit above a substantial charge for members’ remuneration. RBB records no such charge below operating profit; Oxera’s accounts for the year to 30 April 2025 record £8.3m. Their operating margins are 56.3% and 38.4%, respectively. Divide the residual profit available for members by revenue instead, and the figures are 55.6% and 26.5%.

That wider gap partly reflects where remuneration is recorded. It is not a clean measure of whose economists work more productively. Companies may pay working owners through salaries and dividends, while LLPs differ in how they charge and allocate member rewards. The data explorer gives the reported measures; the methodology explains their limits.

Selling the business

Partnership profit is only one way to realise value. In March 2021 McKinsey announced the acquisition of Vivid Economics and Planetrics, bringing economic analysis and climate-scenario modelling into its sustainability work. The announcement did not disclose the price.

The subsequent legal history is distinct from that business acquisition. Vivid Economics Limited entered members’ voluntary liquidation on 10 August 2023, with Steven Sherry and Jen Whatcott appointed as practitioners. The company was dissolved on 27 May 2026, following its final-meeting return. Those events establish what happened to the legal entity; they do not put a value on the team, software or client relationships McKinsey acquired.

PA’s remaining stake

PA Consulting offers a disclosed price, on a much broader business. Jacobs announced terms in January 2026 to acquire the interest it did not already own: about £1.216bn upfront, plus £75m deferred for two years. The acquisition completed on 20 March 2026. Jacobs said on 23 March that it could pay the deferred amount in shares, cash or a combination.

The announcement valued the whole of PA at £3.05bn, or 13 times expected calendar-2025 adjusted EBITDA—earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, with further company adjustments. Including estimated savings from the combination reduced the multiple to 12.3. These are transaction measures based on expected earnings, not realised returns to individual employees.

Jacobs had previously acquired 65% of PA in a deal announced in November 2020 at a £1.825bn enterprise value. Jacobs reported approximately 71% ownership before the March 2026 buyout. Each remaining holder’s proceeds depend on the equity terms and allocation. The £3.05bn whole-business valuation describes a different measure from the consideration for the remaining shares.

PA’s remaining shareholders realised value through a sale to an existing investor. Vivid’s purchase shows another form of integration. Neither transaction isolates the value of UK economics consultancy as a separate activity.

Backing a new firm

Econic’s launch on 19 February 2025 offered a different route. Its release named nine founding partners, including Jonathan Orszag, and described Catherine Barron as managing partner responsible for day-to-day operations. Goldman Sachs Alternatives and the Willig and Ordover families were among its investors. Outside capital was backing the creation of a new consultancy.

FTI supplied evidence of the potential cost of losing senior economists. On its February 2025 earnings call, FTI warned that departures from Compass Lexecon’s American competition practice could damage revenue and profit. Management used an earlier $35m decline in adjusted EBITDA as a tentative guide to the possible order of magnitude.

FTI’s first-quarter results subsequently reported Economic Consulting revenue of $179.9m, down 12.1%, and billable headcount down 6.6%. They attributed the revenue fall primarily to lower demand. Segment adjusted EBITDA edged up from $14.2m to $14.4m. Those results give context; they do not measure a $35m loss caused by Econic.

The ownership question is familiar from RBB’s formation: how much of a consultancy’s value rests with the institution, and how much can leave with its experts? FTI’s warning explains why a buyer or investor must consider both. It does not tell us how many clients followed the departing people.

The work behind the figures

Litigation gives one concrete example of demand for these skills. In July 2016 the European Commission imposed €2.93bn in trucks-cartel fines. MAN, which revealed the cartel, received full immunity and paid no fine. Scania was fined a further €880.52m in September 2017. Damages claims followed.

Royal Mail and BT’s claims against DAF were tried between May and June 2022, with judgment in February 2023. The Tribunal records about 48 expert reports and 12 days of expert evidence in a 25-day trial. James Harvey of Economic Insight appeared for the claimants and Damien Neven of Compass Lexecon for DAF. The Tribunal assessed a 5% overcharge using a “broad axe”. It criticised the influence of clients’ commercial interests on both sides’ conclusions, while finding that their positions remained within a robustly arguable range.

The case shows how much economic work a dispute can require. The financial figures examined here do not isolate the resulting fees, so they cannot tell us how much damages litigation contributed to the firms’ revenue growth.

What would change the economics?

Further acquisitions could give existing owners a route to cash out. More funded entrants could offer senior economists a different bargain over profit and control. Both possibilities follow from transactions already described. Their success may depend in part on whether clients and colleagues follow the people being hired. Revenue growth alone will not reveal who benefits: future accounts will also need to show how many owners share it.
